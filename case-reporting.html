Reporting & analysis

Reporting should explain what changed and what to do next.

I review SEO, email, and website performance in context. The output is not just a table of numbers. It is a clear statement of what moved, what may explain it, and what action deserves attention next.

SEO visibilityEmail performanceAccount reviewRecommendations
Framework

Separate observation from interpretation.

01ObserveWhat changed in rankings, traffic, opens, clicks, or other tracked activity?
02ValidateMake sure the data and reporting narrative agree.
03ContextCheck query, page, audience, device, location, timing, and campaign activity.
04InterpretDecide whether the signal is meaningful, stable, weak, or needs investigation.
05ActRecommend what to repeat, fix, test, or prioritize next.

SEO updates

Keyword positions, branded and local visibility, service intent, search-volume context, and campaign movement.

Email performance

Newsletter and follow-up comparison, opens, clicks, unsubscribes, engaged audiences, and next-step recommendations.

Underperforming accounts

Review the account beyond a single KPI and look for content, technical, local, authority, or execution issues that could explain weak performance.

Website signals

Traffic, acquisition, page activity, events, and content performance where the available analytics support the analysis.

Principle

Do not force a positive story when the data does not support one.

Good reporting can still be constructive without hiding a weak period. If there is no measurable improvement, the report should say that calmly, explain what is known, and focus the team on the next useful action.

Client-specific figures and private reports are not reproduced publicly. The portfolio shows the reporting method and the types of decisions I support.
