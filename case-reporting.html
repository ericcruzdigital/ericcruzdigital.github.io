Marketing Reporting & Analytics

I have prepared and reviewed monthly marketing reports that combine web analytics, keyword movement, page performance, and email campaign data. The public case study shows the workflow and reporting dimensions while keeping client-identifying material private.

Typical reporting inputs

  • Traffic acquisition by source / medium.
  • New-user acquisition.
  • GA4 events and engagement.
  • Most-viewed pages and user activity.
  • Keyword positions and movement.
  • Email opens, clicks, unsubscribes, and follow-up engagement.
Workflow

From raw exports to a client-readable update.

The goal is not to paste dashboards into a document. It is to decide what changed, what matters, and what should happen next.

01CollectGA4, keyword tracker, email stats, page data.
02ComparePeriod-over-period changes and movement.
03ValidateCheck anomalies, context, and source quality.
04ExplainSummarize patterns in plain language.
05PrioritizeTurn observations into next actions.
What I analyze

Reporting dimensions I have worked with.

These categories are based on actual monthly-review and campaign-reporting documents in my work files.

Acquisition

Sessions and engaged sessions by source / medium, direct, organic, referral, and campaign traffic.

Users & engagement

Total/new users, user engagement, page views, event counts, and page-level activity.

Conversions & events

Lead-generation events, form starts/submits, and other key engagement events where tracking exists.

SEO visibility

Keyword positions, movement, top gainers, strong performers, and terms approaching better visibility.

Email performance

Recipients, opens, clicks, unsubscribe activity, follow-up performance, and engaged segments.

Page performance

High-traffic pages, active users, event activity, and content or landing pages showing meaningful change.

Reporting standard

How I avoid overclaiming.

Marketing reports can easily confuse correlation with causation. My preferred approach is to describe the observed change first, then separate interpretation from fact.

  • Fact: a channel or keyword changed during the comparison period.
  • Interpretation: why that movement may matter to the business.
  • Next action: what should be checked, repeated, or improved.
