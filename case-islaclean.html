Independent practice project

IslaClean Palawan

A fictional local cleaning brand built as a controlled SEO environment. The purpose is to practice implementation honestly: research, architecture, on-page work, technical QA, conversion-event handling, and reporting — without pretending the site is a client.

Open live website ↗Project transparency page

What exists now

  • 10-page responsive HTML/CSS/JavaScript website.
  • Separate routine, deep-cleaning, move-in/out, local, and informational intents.
  • Unique titles, meta descriptions, canonical URLs, and page-specific H1s.
  • Contextual internal links between services, service area, blog, and contact paths.
  • Demo CTA and form interactions pushed to dataLayer.
  • Intentional noindex because the business is fictional.
Project architecture

Built around intent, not one generic services page.

The structure gives each service and content purpose a clear destination while keeping navigation simple.

Commercial

Service pages

House Cleaning, Deep Cleaning, and Move-In / Move-Out Cleaning each answer a different service intent.

Local

Service area

A dedicated Puerto Princesa service-area page avoids repeating location copy on every page while providing a local internal-link target.

Informational

Blog content

A room-by-room deep-cleaning guide demonstrates informational content that naturally links into relevant services.

Implementation choices

Why the site is useful in an interview.

I can open the code, explain the architecture, show the metadata, follow the internal links, inspect the demo event handling, and change the site. That makes the project stronger than a static mockup because the implementation is inspectable.

Deliberate decisions

  • No fake business identity: no fabricated address, reviews, customers, rankings, phone number, or traffic claims.
  • No accidental indexing: each page stays noindex while the brand is fictional.
  • Simple front end: static HTML/CSS/JS keeps the site fast, portable, and easy to audit.
  • Measurement-ready practice: CTA and form events are structured for later GA4/GTM testing.
